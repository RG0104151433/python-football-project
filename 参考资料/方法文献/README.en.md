# Method references and their roles

[中文](README.md) | **English**


This directory preserves methodological sources, their actual reading/download status and how the study used them. The research and course submissions have finished; old “not yet implemented/submitted” statements in dated notes are not current project status.

## Source inventory

Use [sources.json](sources.json) and the files in `原文/` to check whether a full local text was obtained. A URL, abstract, search excerpt, blocked response or failed download is not a saved full paper. Original access dates and version identifiers remain historical; this documentation audit does not claim fresh online verification of every source.

## Dependence and uncertainty

- Aronow, Samii and Assenova, *Cluster-Robust Variance Estimation for Dyadic Data*: shared-team, repeated and directed observations. It does not by itself justify every normal-approximation interval.
- Tabord-Meehan, *Inference with Dyadic Data*: asymptotic approximation, imbalance and correlation issues; distinguish revision year from original submission/publication.
- Canen and Sugiura, *Inference in Linear Dyadic Data Models with Network Spillovers*: dependence beyond shared teams, not an automatically adopted replacement estimator.
- Cameron and Miller, *A Practitioner's Guide to Cluster-Robust Inference*: dependent observations and finite-sample issues; generic clustering does not replace the dyadic structure.

The full study later developed and validated its own conditional model; the presence of a reference does not mean its estimator was used unchanged. See [Q4](../../研究/04_共同可靠性/README.en.md) and [Q5](../../研究/05_赛前实力/README.en.md).

## Standardization and temporal evaluation

Klein and Schoenborn's CDC standardization note motivates common reference composition, separately from uncertainty estimation. CDC adjustment pages and NIST interval material provide definitions/background, not automatic treatment of correlated matches. The NIST local page concerns Wilson intervals. The forecasting textbook distinguishes time-based validation from retrospective standardization; its blocked local download was not counted as acquired text.

## Geographic comparisons

UNSD M49 supplies statistical geographic labels, not FIFA membership, historical continuity or proof that a subregion shares football strength or climate. Positivity/support references explain why missing strata cannot be filled with invented rates and why restricting the sample changes the estimand.

McSharry's altitude study and Liu's Asian qualification study illustrate football questions involving opponents/environment or subregions. Their outcomes, coefficients and intervals are not transferred directly. The recorded McSharry PDF download failed; Liu was read at public abstract/metadata level, not claimed as full-text review.

The adopted study uses geography rather than an economic explanatory model. [Q3](../../研究/03_地域差异/README.en.md) states Turkey/Europe, Iran/Western Asia and Australia/Southeastern Asia as project conventions. M49 source originals are in the [shared source folder](../../data/external/geography_sources/m49-20261026/README.en.md).
