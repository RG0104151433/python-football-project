# Revised English speaking outline — 17 November

12 main screens plus one optional appendix. Target about 10–12 minutes; this is an outline, not a timed rehearsal.

1. Opening (30s): “We saved these probabilities on 2 November. Today, the important question is what we could have said before kick-off.” Do not call a completed match a future prediction.
2. Data (40s): Point to each ring. Explain different denominators and overlapping uses.
3. Era/type (45s): Add the two match types. “The archive shows differences; it does not yet explain their causes.”
4. Geography + reliability (50s): Hover one Asian and one European comparison. Open the Q4 callout. Some conditional ranges remain inconclusive.
5. Rating considerations (40s): Venue, opponents, friendlies, surprising results, evolving teams and uncertainty.
6. Definition + map (70s): Show Brazil then Nepal or Luxembourg. The score is a shared latent scale, not a FIFA rank. Whiskers represent uncertain ability, not the next scoreline.
7. Predictors (60s): Reveal strength, then context. Earlier bars stay visible. Show annual fluctuations, not a story of inevitable improvement.
8. History (50s): Advance toward lower log loss. Cumulative is retained among tested alternatives, not proved universally best.
9. Forecast ledger (45s): Switch three-way/ranges and explain these are distinct model branches.
10. Outcome check (60s): State the correctness rule before showing totals. Pending is excluded. Import verified results before the talk; never invent missing outcomes.
11. Examples (45s): Compare one correct, one incorrect or pending case when available. Probability forecasts can be wrong on individual matches.
12. Conclusion (40s): Follow the pipeline, state three findings, invite classmates to the linked repository.

Optional appendix: show only if asked how prediction and updating differ. Learn from the observed result, never from the predicted label.
